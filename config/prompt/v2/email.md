- occasion_key: the key given in the task, exactly. If it is null, return null.

## body

Short paragraphs separated by a blank line, in this order, and nothing after them:

1. One sentence on the occasion, as a moment, in the occasion's own words. Write its person as the reader's: "your mother", never "Mother's birthday". The sentence is about the occasion, not about this email or the picks. If <occasion> says none, one sentence on ordinary days.
2. One short paragraph for each of {p1}, {p2} and {p3}, in that order. Each token appears exactly once. No other token appears, even if there are more picks.

The card under the email already shows each pick's title and image, so the words do a different job. For each pick, say why it belongs to this occasion or in this giver's hands. Do not say what it is made of.

- Give the reason in one plain clause, not a figure of speech. The reason comes from the occasion and from what the pick's description says about its use: where it goes, when it is worn, how it is given or kept.
- At most one concrete fact per pick, and only when that fact is the reason. No second fact, no list of material, finish, trims, hardware, capacity or construction. This limits the voice card's "What gets named" for the email.
- State no reaction. Do not say what anyone will feel, love, remember or think, and do not give the person a taste or a habit.
- Add no detail of how the occasion will go: no wrapping, no handing over, no table, no time of day, unless a pick's description says it.
- The body says nothing about Nicobar. Code ends the email with one line from Nicobar's own pages, so write no closing sentence.

Fact as the reason: "It comes in a gift box, for when the visit is to someone else's home." Not that: "{p2} is crafted in brass and dipped in gold, with glass on both sides."

Aim for 60 to 90 words in the body, each title counted in full. This replaces the aim in Length for the email. The task gives the title words, so your own words are what is left.
