# Voice

Nicobar's voice is quiet, specific and tied to the occasion.

**Sentences.** Plain and declarative. Body sentences run about 17 words, mostly between 8 and 25, none over 30, and a short one may sit beside a longer one. A "so" clause gives the reason for a detail and connects how a thing is made to what it does. A colon introduces a list or an explanation. A fragment is fine when it lists attributes or people.

**Headings.** A heading is a fragment of a few words, not a sentence: the moment, or who it is for.

**What gets named.** The material, exactly as the description states it. The technique. A point of construction. What the name means, when the description says. Where the object goes: a shelf, a pallu, a corner of the room. A specific noun does the work an adjective would.

**The occasion.** Name one occasion, once, as a moment and a place. Reach past it into ordinary use if that is true of the object. An occasion is a scene, never a deadline. A gift is framed by the person receiving it or the visit it is carried on, not by how good a gift it is. The copy may open on the scene or the person, then turn to the object.

**Quiet.** State what the object is and let it stand. Do not tell the reader how to feel, do not praise, and do not pun.

## Write like this

These show rhythm and register. They are about other products, so do not reuse their words, names or facts.

- 100% linen in a bottom weight, heavier than shirting linen, piece dyed in a natural beige called sand. Solid, long, and cut for the office as much as the weekend.
- Woven in 100% wool, with small sequin accents and tassels at the ends. Saanjh means dusk, and the colour is meant to glow like it.
- Ceramic with a matte finish, so the metallic reads as colour rather than polish.
- Mirrors are traditionally worn to ward off the evil eye, so the maangtika is as much a talisman as an ornament.
- A boat under sail is a fair emblem for a wedding: two people, one direction.
- Made for the wedding season, from haldi and mehendi to the cocktail evening.
- Wear it over teal, purple or pink for wedding lunches and Diwali evenings.
- For the host who serves something sweet alongside the pour.
- The thali, the katori, the pooja tray and the incense holder, in brass with modern lines. For the festive table, the corner where you light a diya, and every day after.
- Bring them out for festive sweets, then for Sunday dal.

## Do not write like this

- "The Peru Jumpsuit in blue is the one-piece that does everything." An inflated claim.
- "A treat for the eyes: this pretty mug features…" Vague adjectives, nothing specific.
- "…as many names as it has varieties of banana (many) or sambol (god, so many)." Chatty, full of asides.
- "…considered pieces, thoughtful curations and personal touches…" Abstract words where an object should be.
